# 3D BORE Content Series Brief

Brief for an AI agent (or human creator) producing content for 3D BORE. Covers three recurring series: **MITOS ATAU FAKTA**, **BEDAH PROYEK**, and **BENERIN YUK**.

Items marked **[ASSUMPTION]** are not confirmed by the brand owner. Ask before treating them as final.

---

## 0. How to use this document

1. Read sections 1 to 3 first. They apply to every series.
2. Then read the section for the series you are working on (4, 5 or 6).
3. Follow the deliverable format in section 7.
4. Check your work against the checklist in section 8 before handing it back.

If something is missing or ambiguous, ask a question. Do not invent facts, numbers, prices or test results.

---

## 1. Brand context

**Brand:** 3D BORE, an online manufacturing brand offering 3D printing and CNC services.

**Mission:** Making manufacturing more open to anyone. Precision engineering ordered online, with open source hardware and open knowledge.

**Design vibe:** Sharp, Minimal, Modern.
- Sharp: high-contrast geometry, crisp edges, no sloppiness.
- Minimal: remove visual noise so structure and function speak.
- Modern: fast, forward-thinking.
- Rule of thumb: if an element does not serve precision, speed or openness, remove it.

**Business goal of the content:** educate and build trust first, then convert viewers into customers. Every series should end with a soft, natural pointer to 3D BORE's services, never a hard sell.

**Audience [ASSUMPTION]:**
- Primary: Indonesian hobbyists, students, makers and first-time 3D printing users.
- Secondary: small businesses (UMKM), product designers and hardware startups who need prototypes or small-batch parts.

**Language:** Indonesian, casual and conversational (the way a friendly engineer talks, not a corporate brochure). English technical terms are fine when they are the common term (infill, layer adhesion, tolerance, filament). Series titles stay as given.

---

## 2. Voice and tone

Persona: **a sharp engineer who loves sharing what they know.** *(Marked "draft" in brand guidelines; treat as provisional.)*

| Trait | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Direct | Short sentences, active verbs, no filler. Get to the point in the first 3 seconds. |
| Open | Share process, prices and know-how. Credit every creator whose design appears. |
| Precise | Use real numbers: temperatures, tolerances, materials, print times, lead times. |
| Friendly | Approachable to first-timers. Never condescending, never gatekeeping. |

**Do:** "Part kamu jadi dalam 3 hari. Mau lebih kuat? Ganti ke PETG."
**Don't:** "Kami dengan bangga menghadirkan solusi additive manufacturing terdepan untuk meningkatkan pengalaman Anda!"

Avoid: hype words, empty superlatives, clickbait that the content does not pay off, fear-mongering, talking down to viewers.

## 3. Shared rules for all series

### 3.1 Accuracy and honesty
- Every factual claim (material properties, temperatures, strength, safety) must be verifiable. Cite or note the source in the production notes, even if it is not shown on screen.
- Do not fabricate test results. If a demo or test is shown, it must be real and reproducible. Describe the test method so it can be repeated.
- Be honest about conditions. Results depend on printer, settings, orientation and material brand. Say so instead of making absolute claims.
- Do not invent prices, lead times or customer details. Use placeholders like `[HARGA]` or `[LEAD TIME]` for the team to fill.

### 3.2 Credit and ownership
- Always credit the creator and state the license when showing someone else's design (for example from Makerworld, Printables or Thingiverse). Show the creator name and source on screen and in the caption.
- Customer projects (Bedah Proyek) require **customer permission** before publishing. Offer anonymization if they prefer.

### 3.3 Visual identity (from the 3D BORE design system)

**Colors.** Cobalt leads, Navy anchors, Chartreuse energizes, Blush punctuates.

| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary brand | Cobalt Surge 500 | `#3065EF` |
| Foundation | Midnight Navy 500 | `#001848` |
| Accent | Electric Chartreuse 500 | `#DBEB42` |
| Spark | Blush Pop 500 | `#FB4988` |
| Neutrals | Void Black `#0C0C0C`, White `#FFFFFF` | |

Approximate proportion of any layout: Cobalt 35%, Navy 30%, White 20%, Chartreuse 10%, Blush 5%. Blush is for rare emphasis only and never a background.

**Accessible pairs only:** White on Cobalt, White on Navy, Navy on Chartreuse, Chartreuse on Navy, Navy on Blush, Cobalt on White. Never white text on Chartreuse or Blush. Never Chartreuse text on white.

**Typography.** Single family: **Geom** (weights 300 to 700). Fall back to a geometric sans-serif. Sentence-case headlines. All caps only for small labels (eyebrows, tags, buttons). Never use a second typeface. Never center long paragraphs.

**Imagery.** Real parts, real machines, real hands. Macro shots with one sharp edge in focus, a printer mid-print against dark navy, maker hands in natural light, flat lays on Chartreuse or white. Show the layer, the toolpath and the finished part. No generic stock scenes.

**Graphic language.**
- "Supersonic cones": sharp wedges radiating from a point, in Chartreuse, Blush, Navy, light Cobalt and White on a Cobalt field. Use **one** graphic device per layout, cropped by the edge.
- Corners only from radius tokens (small chips 8, cards 16, large containers 32, pills fully rounded).
- Icons: simple, geometric, 24 px grid, 2 px round-cap stroke, one color.

**Layout.** One idea per post or frame. Generous whitespace. Logo in a corner. Logo minimum width 120 px on screen. Logo clearspace on every side equals the height of the "o" in BORE. Never stretch, recolor, outline or add effects to the logo. Use White or Electric logo on dark or Cobalt fields, Navy logo on light fields.

**Series naming style.** Series titles are written in ALL CAPS (they act as labels). Headlines inside content stay sentence case.

### 3.4 Platforms and formats **[ASSUMPTION]**
Not specified by the owner. Default plan:
- Short vertical video (9:16) for TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.
- Optional longer horizontal cut (16:9) on YouTube for Bedah Proyek.
- Carousel or still-image version for Instagram where useful.

### 3.5 Call to action (CTA) principles
- One CTA per piece, placed at the end.
- It must feel like the natural next step, not an ad.
- Rotate between: "Upload file kamu ke 3D BORE untuk instant quote" (adapt to the actual quote flow), "Tanya di komentar", "Simpan buat nanti", "Kirim ke temen yang butuh".
- Confirm the real URL, quote tool name and promo details with the team. Use `[LINK]` placeholders until confirmed.

## 4. Series A: MITOS ATAU FAKTA

### 4.1 Concept
Short, punchy episodes that test one common belief about 3D printing, CNC or manufacturing and give a clear verdict backed by a real demo, a number or a source.

### 4.2 Goals
- Establish 3D BORE as a credible, knowledgeable source (authority).
- Highly shareable and saveable content that drives reach.
- Teach beginners to avoid costly mistakes, which leads naturally to better file submissions.

### 4.3 Audience
Beginners and intermediate hobbyists who have heard conflicting advice on forums and social media.

### 4.4 Format
- Length: 30 to 60 seconds [ASSUMPTION].
- Cadence: 1 episode per week [ASSUMPTION].
- Style: fast-paced, one idea, visual proof on screen.

### 4.5 Episode structure

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3s | **Hook.** State the myth as a bold claim. | Example: "PLA nggak bisa dipakai di luar ruangan. Mitos atau fakta?" |
| 3 to 8s | **Where it comes from.** Why people believe it. | One sentence. |
| 8 to 35s | **The test or evidence.** Demo, measurement, data or credible source. | Show the real thing. Include real numbers. |
| 35 to 45s | **Verdict.** Stamp: MITOS, FAKTA or SEBAGIAN BENAR. | Big on-screen verdict, brand colors. |
| 45 to 55s | **The nuance and the practical tip.** | "Jadi kapan ini bener? Kalau ..." |
| Last 5s | **Soft CTA.** | Link the tip to a 3D BORE service or material option. |

### 4.6 Verdict system
- **MITOS** (Cobalt or Navy stamp): the claim is false.
- **FAKTA** (Chartreuse stamp on Navy): the claim is true.
- **SEBAGIAN BENAR** (Blush used sparingly, or Cobalt): true only under certain conditions. This is likely the most common verdict. Always state the conditions.

### 4.7 Episode idea bank
These are hypotheses with a preliminary verdict. **Every one must be verified with sources or a real test before filming.**

| Claim | Preliminary verdict | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| "PLA nggak bisa dipakai di luar ruangan" | Sebagian benar | PLA softens at relatively low temperatures. Heat (for example inside a parked car in Indonesian weather) is the real risk, not "outdoors" itself. Compare with PETG and ASA. |
| "Part 3D print itu pasti lemah" | Sebagian benar | Strength depends on material, wall count, orientation and layer adhesion. Show a load test along vs. across layers. |
| "Infill makin tinggi, makin kuat" | Mostly mitos | Diminishing returns. Walls and perimeters often matter more. Test 20% vs 100% infill with real weight and strength numbers. |
| "Resin selalu beracun" | Sebagian benar | Uncured resin is an irritant and needs gloves and ventilation. Properly cured parts are a different story. Cover safe handling. |
| "Filament nggak perlu dikeringkan" | Mitos (for many materials) | Humidity in Indonesia matters. Show a wet vs. dry filament print comparison. |
| "Print lebih lambat selalu lebih bagus" | Sebagian benar | Quality vs. speed trade-offs, and where slow speed actually helps. |
| "Printer FDM harus pakai enclosure" | Sebagian benar | Mostly relevant for materials like ABS/ASA. Depends on material. |
| "CNC itu cuma untuk part metal" | Mitos | CNC also machines plastics, wood and composites. Show examples. |
| "3D printing cuma cocok untuk prototype" | Mitos | End-use parts, jigs, fixtures, small-batch production. |

### 4.8 Visual direction
- Opening card: eyebrow label "MITOS ATAU FAKTA" in Label style, claim in H1 or Display weight on a Cobalt or Navy field.
- Verdict stamp as the single graphic device of the episode.
- Real footage of the test, with macro shots and a clear on-screen number.
- Consistent end card with the logo.

### 4.9 Do and don't
- **Do:** show proof, state conditions, keep verdicts honest even if "sebagian benar" is less dramatic.
- **Don't:** shame competitors, forums or individual creators who spread the myth. Don't call it a myth if it is only partly true.

## 5. Series B: BEDAH PROYEK

### 5.1 Concept
A case-study series following a **real customer project** from CAD file to finished part: the problem, the design decisions, the process settings and the result.

### 5.2 Goals
- Prove capability with real work (social proof).
- Show the process openly (supports the "open knowledge" mission).
- Educate viewers on design-for-manufacturing and material choice.
- Attract customers with similar needs (UMKM, startups, students).

### 5.3 Audience
Prospective customers who need to see that 3D BORE can handle a project like theirs, plus makers who enjoy process content.

### 5.4 Format
- Short version: 60 to 90 seconds vertical [ASSUMPTION].
- Long version (optional): 5 to 8 minutes horizontal for YouTube [ASSUMPTION].
- Cadence: 1 to 2 per month, depending on available projects [ASSUMPTION].

### 5.5 Prerequisites before producing an episode
1. Written permission from the customer (or confirmed anonymization).
2. Customer-approved details: what may be shown (part, function, brand name) and what may not (confidential specs, pricing).
3. Access to the original CAD file or screenshots, photos or footage of the process, and the final part.
4. Verified data: material, printer or machine, layer height, infill, print time, post-processing, and any measured tolerance. Use only data the team provides.

### 5.6 Episode structure

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. The brief** | Who the customer is (or "seorang maker/startup"), and what problem they needed solved. |
| **2. The challenge** | What made it hard: tight tolerance, strength, heat, complex geometry, deadline. |
| **3. Design and decisions** | What was changed or recommended and why (orientation, wall thickness, splitting the part, material choice). Show before and after if the file was adjusted. |
| **4. The process** | Printer or CNC machine, settings, time-lapse of the build. Include real numbers. |
| **5. Post-processing** | Supports removal, sanding, finishing, assembly, inspection. |
| **6. The result** | The finished part in use. Measured results (fit, tolerance, weight, cost or time saved) where available. |
| **7. Takeaway and CTA** | One lesson viewers can reuse, then soft CTA to upload a file for a quote. |

### 5.7 Required data sheet (fill per episode)
```
Project name:
Customer (named / anonymized / permission status):
Problem statement:
Part function and environment (load, heat, outdoors, food contact, etc.):
Process: FDM / resin / CNC / other
Machine:
Material:
Key settings (layer height, walls, infill, speeds, tool, feeds):
Design changes made (and why):
Print or machine time:
Post-processing steps:
Result / measured outcomes:
Cost or lead time (if approved to share):
Lessons learned:
Footage and photo assets available:
```

### 5.8 Visual direction
- Open on the finished part (hero macro shot), then rewind to the problem.
- Use split-screen for before and after, or CAD vs. real part.
- Spec rows on screen (Label style: MATERIAL, LAYER HEIGHT, TIME) using brand colors, minimal and clean.
- Time-lapse of the build on a dark Navy background for contrast.

### 5.9 Do and don't
- **Do:** be transparent about trade-offs and what you would change next time.
- **Don't:** publish without permission, exaggerate results, hide failures (a small honest hiccup builds trust).

## 6. Series C: BENERIN YUK
*(formerly "Repair Kuy")*

### 6.1 Concept
Fixing everyday broken things (a lost knob, a cracked bracket, a discontinued part) by designing and 3D printing a custom replacement. Practical, relatable and satisfying.

### 6.2 Goals
- Show real, everyday value of 3D printing to non-makers.
- Highly shareable "fix it" content that widens the audience.
- Encourage viewers to send their own broken-part problems (community and lead generation).
- Reinforce the open-knowledge mission by sharing design files when appropriate.

### 6.3 Audience
General public, homeowners, hobbyists and anyone with a broken item and no spare part available.

### 6.4 Format
- Length: 45 to 90 seconds vertical [ASSUMPTION].
- Cadence: 2 to 4 per month [ASSUMPTION].
- Style: problem, quick measure, design, print, fit, "it works".

### 6.5 Episode structure

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 4s | **Hook.** Show the broken item and the annoyance. | "Knob kompor patah, spare part-nya udah nggak dijual." |
| 4 to 15s | **Diagnose and measure.** | Calipers on screen. Show real dimensions. |
| 15 to 35s | **Design.** Quick CAD or a modified open-source model. | Credit any downloaded design. |
| 35 to 55s | **Print.** Time-lapse, material choice and why. | Include print time and material. |
| 55 to 75s | **Fit test and result.** | The satisfying moment: it works. Show before and after. |
| Last 5 to 10s | **Tip and soft CTA.** | "Punya barang rusak? Kirim ke kami / upload file-nya." |

### 6.6 Material and safety rules (important)
Material choice must match the use. State the reason in every episode.
- **Heat:** parts near stoves, ovens, hot cars or heaters need heat-tolerant materials. Standard PLA can soften and deform in heat. Do not use it for hot environments.
- **Outdoors or UV:** consider materials suited to sun and weather.
- **Flexible parts:** consider TPU for grips, gaskets, feet and bumpers.
- **Food contact:** do not claim a printed part is "food safe" unless it is verified for that material, process and finish. When in doubt, say it is not recommended for direct food contact.
- **Do NOT feature repairs of safety-critical parts.** Examples: gas regulators or gas fittings, brakes, helmets and protective gear, load-bearing climbing or lifting equipment, live electrical components, child car seats. If such an item comes up, advise using an official replacement part. Show a safe alternative instead.
- Add a short on-screen note when relevant: "Cek dulu kecocokan material dengan kondisi pemakaian."

### 6.7 Episode idea bank
- Knob kompor, kipas, atau radio yang hilang.
- Engsel atau penutup baterai remote yang patah.
- Bracket atau holder yang retak (rak, kamera, lampu).
- Kancing atau gesper tas yang rusak.
- Kaki kursi, kaki meja, atau foot pad yang hilang.
- Gear plastik pada mainan atau alat rumah tangga.
- Tutup atau klip pada peralatan dapur non-panas.
- Spare part untuk barang vintage atau discontinued.
- Custom organizer: holder sikat gigi, kabel organizer, hook.

### 6.8 Community loop
- Invite viewers to send their broken-item photos with measurements.
- Pick one per cycle to fix on camera, with the owner's permission and credit.
- Make the design file available when it is the team's own design and the owner agrees [ASSUMPTION: confirm sharing policy and license, for example Creative Commons].

### 6.9 Visual direction
- Opening frame: the broken item in sharp macro, real hands, natural light.
- Before and after split.
- Calipers and measurement overlays as the "precision" signal.
- Flat lay on white or Chartreuse for the finished part.

### 6.10 Do and don't
- **Do:** be practical and honest about durability ("ini tahan sekitar ... untuk pemakaian ringan").
- **Don't:** promise a fix will last forever, skip the material and heat check, or feature unsafe repairs.

## 7. Deliverable format (for the agent)

When asked to develop content for a series, deliver the following unless told otherwise.

### 7.1 Episode plan
```
Series:
Episode title (working):
Hook line (first 3 seconds):
Key message (one sentence):
Facts and numbers needing verification (with sources):
Proof or demo required (what to film or test):
CTA:
```

### 7.2 Script table
| Time | Visual (shot description) | Voiceover (Bahasa Indonesia, casual) | On-screen text |
|---|---|---|---|

Keep voiceover short and spoken-style. One idea per line. Match the voice in section 2.

### 7.3 Caption pack
- Caption: 1 to 3 short sentences plus a question to spark comments.
- Credits line (creator and license for any third-party design, or customer credit if approved).
- Hashtags: 5 to 8, a mix of broad (#3dprinting) and Indonesian or niche tags. Do not use misleading hashtags.
- CTA line with `[LINK]` placeholder.

### 7.4 Production notes
- Shot list with required props, machines and materials.
- Assets needed from the team (files, footage, photos, permissions).
- Open questions for the team.

## 8. Pre-delivery checklist

- [ ] Hook lands within 3 seconds.
- [ ] Every factual claim is verified, with a source noted.
- [ ] No fabricated numbers, tests, prices or lead times (placeholders used where unknown).
- [ ] Creator and license credited for any third-party design.
- [ ] Customer permission confirmed (Bedah Proyek).
- [ ] No safety-critical repair featured (Benerin Yuk).
- [ ] Material choice matches use and conditions, with the reason stated.
- [ ] Voice is direct, open, precise and friendly. No hype or filler.
- [ ] Brand colors and contrast pairs are respected. Geom typeface, sentence-case headlines.
- [ ] One graphic device and one idea per piece.
- [ ] One soft CTA at the end.
- [ ] Items marked [ASSUMPTION] or "draft" flagged back to the team.
